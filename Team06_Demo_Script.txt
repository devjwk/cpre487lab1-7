[English Script]
On the challenge side: just as Lab 6 showed how loop and thread overhead can hurt CPU speed, Lab 7 showed how sensitive hardware is to addressing and memory timing.

The two hardest parts in Lab 7 were calculating the 5-loop signed address jumps in index_gen without multipliers, and timing the BRAM Read-Modify-Write cycle for MaxPooling in output_storage.
In fact, delaying conv_complete by just one clock cycle was critical so the final pooled byte finished writing to BRAM before the accelerator went idle.

Stepping back across Labs 5, 6, and 7, my biggest takeaway is that data movement dominates compute performance—whether it was per-packet FIFO overhead in Lab 5, unit-stride cache locality giving a 5.4x speedup in Lab 6, or double-buffered on-chip BRAM avoiding DDR copies in Lab 7.

[SLIDE 4] Team 06 – Project Idea
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[English Script]
Originally, my project proposal was to add DMA transfers to the accelerator, because in Lab 5 my bottleneck was the AXI-FIFO transfer.

However, as the instructor feedback pointed out, Lab 7 already includes AXI CDMA—and as I showed on Slide 2, my synthesis results confirm that on-chip BRAM is now the primary bottleneck at 49%, while LUTs and DSPs are under 10%.

So I pivoted my project to Weight and Activation Compression with Sparse Zero-Skipping to attack that BRAM and data-movement bottleneck directly.
Because ReLU turns all negative activations into zeros, I will compress zero-heavy activations and weights using lossless Run-Length or Bitmask encoding before sending them over DMA.

In hardware, I'll add a lightweight decoder between the BRAM and the MACs, and update the index generator and MAC enable logic to skip zero-value cycles on Conv2—allowing me to save both on-chip BRAM and inference latency without losing accuracy.

Thank you, and I'm happy to take any questions!

[한국어 번역]
원래 제 프로젝트 제안서는 Lab 5의 병목이었던 FIFO 통신을 개선하기 위해 가속기에 DMA를 추가하는 것이었습니다.
하지만 교수님 피드백에서 짚어주셨듯 Lab 7에 이미 AXI CDMA가 들어 있고, 앞서 Slide 2 합성 결과에서 보여드렸듯이 LUT와 DSP는 10% 미만인데 온칩 BRAM만 49%를 차지해 이제는 BRAM이 가장 큰 병목이 되었습니다.
그래서 이 BRAM 용량과 데이터 이동 병목을 정면으로 해결하기 위해 주제를 '가중치·활성화 압축 및 희소 제로 스키핑'으로 발전시켰습니다.
ReLU를 거치면 음수가 모두 0이 되어 0이 아주 많아지므로, DMA로 보내기 전에 무손실 Run-Length나 비트마스크 방식으로 데이터를 압축해 전송할 것입니다.
하드웨어에서는 BRAM과 MAC 사이에 가벼운 디코더를 추가하고, 인덱스 생성기와 MAC 제어 로직이 값이 0인 연산 클럭을 건너뛰게(Zero-skipping) 만들어 정확도 손실 없이 Conv2의 BRAM 사용량과 추론 시간을 동시에 줄이고자 합니다.
감사합니다, 질문 받겠습니다!

Q1. How did you split threads in Lab 6, and what SIMD flags did you use?
A1. "I split threads across the output rows (p dimension) using std::thread so threads never write to the same memory. For SIMD, I used -O3 auto-vectorization over the sequential output-channel (m) innermost loop."
(출력 행 p를 기준으로 스레드를 나눠 메모리 충돌을 없앴고, SIMD는 연속된 m 안쪽 루프에 -O3 자동 벡터화를 적용했습니다.)

Q2. How many images did you test for the Lab 6 latency benchmarks?
A2. "I measured single-image full-CNN inference latency on image_0 across all layers so I could directly compare per-image runtime across all tile sizes and thread counts."
(단일 이미지 전체 레이어 추론 시간을 기준으로 각 타일 크기와 스레드 개수별 지연 시간을 비교 측정했습니다.)

Q3. What was your debugging strategy for Lab 7?
A3. "I debugged incrementally in the Vivado testbench by grouping the waveform into the 4 pipeline stages—Index Gen, MACs, Dequantization, and Output Storage—and fixing each stage's fail signal in order. That's how I caught the 1-cycle delay needed on conv_complete for the final BRAM write."
(Vivado 테스트벤치에서 4개 스테이지 순서대로 fail 신호를 하나씩 잡는 단계별 디버깅을 했고, 덕분에 마지막 BRAM 쓰기를 위한 conv_complete 1클럭 지연 문제를 바로 잡았습니다.)

Q4. Will your compression project hurt model accuracy?
A4. "No—Run-Length or Bitmask compression on already-quantized 8-bit zeros is completely lossless, and skipping zero multiplications doesn't change the MAC sum, so accuracy stays identical."
(이미 8비트로 양자화된 데이터에서 0을 압축하고 0 곱셈만 건너뛰는 것은 무손실(lossless)이라 정확도는 100% 동일하게 유지됩니다.)
